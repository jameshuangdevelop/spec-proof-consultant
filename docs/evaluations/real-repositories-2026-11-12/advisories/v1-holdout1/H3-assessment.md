## Decisions

Two opportunities, ordered by near-term usefulness. First establish the dependency/architecture contract needed for safe wrapper tests; second investigate a genuinely bounded integer-state property. Do not force a verifier simply because this is Rust. No bug or security finding is established here.

### O1 — P1: architecture and external-helper contract inventory, then wrapper conformance tests

**Value/cost.** A stable seed *type* does not imply stable output or that all 32 bytes affect every architecture. That distinction and partial-byte filling are more useful integration questions than another fixed-seed vector. The observed tests cover core output and seed zero, but do not by themselves establish wrapper/dependency behavior for mixed API calls and buffer lengths. Initial read-only mapping is low cost; dependency access, target coverage and actual test gaps are unknown.

**Target/requirements.** SmallRng::from_seed/seed_from_u64/try_next_u32/try_next_u64/try_fill_bytes and the two underlying implementations above. **Documented:** fixed seed size (source compatibility comment at `small.rs:82-83`), architecture-dependent/nonportable behavior (`:19-42`), all-zero seed remapping in the inner APIs (`xoshiro*.rs:31-41`). **Inferred proposal:** for this pinned revision and target, SmallRng exactly forwards the selected inner engine initialized from the specified prefix. **Unresolved:** read_words byte order, next_u64_via_u32 word order/error behavior, fill_bytes_via_next_word partial-word consumption, endian conventions, empty-destination behavior and exact trait delegation/blanket implementations. Declared names do not prove these contracts. The missing bodies are ordinary Rust dependencies, **not proven FFI**. Owner approval is absent; the campaign grants read-only advice only.

**Property and domain.** For every `[u8;32]` input seed on the selected pointer width, let inner state be from_seed(seed[0..16]) for widths16/32 or from_seed(all32) for width64. For an identical finite sequence of requested u32/u64/fill operations, wrapper results and final inner state should equal direct selected-engine results. No error value should be producible through these Infallible methods, conditional on actual trait/helper semantics. For integral seeding compare wrapper and the selected inner seed_from_u64 for all u64 seeds. The slice-conversion invariant is LEN∈{16,32}≤32 and conversion length matches the inner array; the unwrap must not panic for these selected configurations. No legal “wrong length seed” is invented: the public type fixes it.

Proposed native samples cover all-zero, one nonzero bit at each byte position, high-bit bytes and all-ones seeds; fill lengths 0..17 crossing 4/8-byte boundaries; clone-before-use; mixed call sequences up to 8 operations. In particular, seeds with zero first16 and nonzero tail must hit the **128-bit zero-seed fallback** on 16/32-bit targets: they must not be excluded as “nonzero seed” exceptions. Two seeds differing only in tail16 are equivalent on these targets in this revision; no corresponding equivalence or inequality is asserted on 64-bit targets. Ordinary wide seed use should not panic; 16-bit compile/runtime support is unknown rather than presumed from a cfg.

Counterexamples: wrapper and selected engine diverge for an identical seed/trace; wrong selected prefix; a panic at the fixed-length slice/conversion; incorrect destination length handling relative to the **approved external helper contract**. Do not require fill(a) concatenated with fill(b) to equal fill(a+b) or next_u64 to equal two u32 calls on both engines: partial-word discarding and the 256-bit engine's high-half u32 behavior may make those false. Empty fill and partial fills are explicit questions until dependency evidence resolves them.

**Method/boundaries.** Noncatalog source contract review then native conformance tests, not proof. Such tests exercise the real helpers once separately authorized; comparing wrapper and inner checks routing/state alignment, not an independent oracle for helper correctness. Broader byte-order/fill functional assertions need the actual resolved rand_core source contract and its authority. No dependency fetch/import/install/run is authorized now; keep missing evidence unknown.

**Alternatives/readiness.** `kani-rust-slices` **1.0.0**, `references/kani.md`, is only a possible later feasibility lead for fixed seeding and bounded buffers, not currently a supported pilot across unknown external helpers. Its conditional assurance is “Enabled checks and assertions over the stated finite harness domain with complete unwinding.” Hard exclusions are FFI, concurrency, async, assembly and dynamic dispatch. Actual rand_core implementations, release/configuration, macros, Rust edition/library support and allocation must be inspected before any compatibility claim. Do not substitute helper success stubs. Native tests avoid proof-model support assumptions but remain sampled evidence. No cross-version/cross-width SmallRng stable-output requirement should be added.

### O2 — P2: nonzero initialization and one-step state preservation

**Impact/value/cost.** Zero is a degenerate state; seeding and transition invariants provide a small precise assurance target beyond two ten-word examples. This does not imply statistical quality, long period, security, unbiased distributions or output nonzero. Cost is uncertain (bitvector multiplications over symbolic u64 seeding may be harder than a single linear state step); prioritize after O1's dependency/authority inventory.

**Requirement/authority.** Nonzero integral seeding is **documented as implementation intent** by comments and debug_assert_ne at `xoshiro128plusplus.rs:60-63` and `xoshiro256plusplus.rs:59-62`; all-zero byte-seed fallback is documented at each `:31-41`. Preservation of nonzero state by the step is an **inferred invariant**, requiring component-owner approval. No upstream or adopter approval is supplied.

**Precise property.** For all 2^64 integral seed inputs, each production seed_from_u64 should terminate after its fixed 2 (128 engine) or 4 (256 engine) SplitMix iterations with some nonzero state word, using modulo-2^64 arithmetic and the implemented split into u32 halves for the 128 engine. Loop invariant: processed slots equal completed SplitMix outputs and the loop consumes a fixed portion of the four-word array; remaining slots are still zero until assigned. This is a fixed finite-domain proposal, not a run.

Separately, for arbitrary four-word states at the engine's exact word width, execute one actual core step (`try_next_u32` for128, `try_next_u64` for256). Under precondition at least one old state word nonzero, at least one new word remains nonzero, no bounds/panic failure occurs, and the result is Ok. Wrapping overflow is intended and must not be forbidden. For an all-zero initial state, include the negative/control case that the transition remains zero and emits zero, rather than assuming it impossible in every construction path. Counterexamples are a seed producing four zero words, a nonzero-to-zero transition, panic or nontermination in the fixed loops. These properties are independent of whether a particular output happens to be zero.

The one-step invariant is **not** by itself a mechanically checked arbitrary-trace theorem, and does not establish a maximal period or correctness against the externally linked reference algorithm. Seed byte decoding still depends on unread read_words. Optional serde derives are an additional constructor surface: no validation hook is visible in the selected structs, so rejection/acceptance of a serialized all-zero state is not established. Do not claim all publicly constructed/deserialized states are nonzero or silently exclude serde to obtain that broader claim. Resolve intended deserialization semantics separately; a core-step invariant with a stated nonzero-state precondition is useful but does not discharge those obligations. SmallRng has no serde derive in its inspected file.

**Method fit.** `kani-rust-arithmetic` **1.0.0**, `references/kani.md`, matches sequential fixed-width arithmetic and finite array state; **compatibility unknown, implementation blocked**. Assurance: “Checks enabled safety properties and assertions for all inputs represented by a harness, subject to complete unwinding, models, assumptions, and tool soundness.” Hard-exclude FFI, concurrency, async, assembly and dynamic dispatch. The core step itself has only fixed indexing, shifts/rotations/wrapping additions; the seed loops use core iterators. Inspect actual tool support for Rust2024/MSRV, these intrinsics, Result/traits/core iterators and dependency compilation before selecting a tool release or claiming reachability isolation. A same-module proof-only harness would need access to private state; do not change production visibility or rewrite the algorithm merely to make a checker accept it. Pin enabled safety checks and retain successful unwinding assertions for all loops.

**Alternatives.** Native properties over varied seeds/states are cheaper sampled evidence and may suffice; manual invertibility reasoning can guide assertions but is not an executed proof. Supported-source deductive adaptation has more correspondence/maintenance cost than this bounded lead; replacement is not authorized. Do not prove security or statistical randomness with either finite model checking or vector tests.
